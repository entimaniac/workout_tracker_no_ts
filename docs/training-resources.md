# Training Resources

This page collects external references we can link to from the app or project docs. The goal is not to duplicate their content, but to point people to solid starting material for workout planning, weight training, stretching, and Jim Wendler's 5/3/1 method.

These are general education links, not personalized medical advice. If someone is returning from injury, managing a chronic condition, or unsure whether a program is safe for them, they should check with a qualified clinician or coach.

## General workout guidance

| Resource | What it covers | When to consult it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [CDC: Adult Activity Overview](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) | High-level weekly activity targets for adults, including aerobic work and muscle-strengthening work. Useful as the baseline "what counts as enough exercise?" reference. | Use this when someone needs the broad health guidelines before choosing a specific program. |
| [CDC: Adding Physical Activity as an Adult](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/adding-adults/index.html) | Practical ways to turn the CDC guidelines into a weekly schedule, with example plans and notes on when to check with a doctor first. | Use this when someone wants sample weekly routines or help turning goals into a realistic plan. |
| [Exercise Is Medicine: Starting an Exercise Program (PDF)](https://www.exerciseismedicine.org/assets/page_documents/8_StartingExProgram.pdf) | Short handout on starting from scratch, building time gradually, and adding strength training twice per week. | Use this when someone is new to training and needs a simple "how do I begin?" handout. |

## Weightlifting and exercise selection

| Resource | What it covers | When to consult it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [NASM Exercise Library](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library) | Large exercise and stretch library with step-by-step instructions and movement descriptions. | Use this when someone knows the name of an exercise and wants a quick refresher on setup, form, or target muscles. |
| [CDC: Growing Stronger - Strength Training for Older Adults (PDF)](https://www.cdc.gov/physicalactivity/downloads/growing_stronger.pdf) | A long-form strength training guide with safety notes, progression ideas, a 12-week workbook, and exercise instruction. It is written for older adults, but much of the structure is broadly useful for conservative beginner programming. | Use this when someone wants a slower, safety-first approach to resistance training or needs a more detailed printable guide. |

## 5/3/1 method

| Resource | What it covers | When to consult it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Jim Wendler: The 5/3/1 Philosophy for Beginners](https://www.jimwendler.com/blogs/jimwendler-com/101065094-5-3-1-for-a-beginner) | Simplified intro to 5/3/1 with beginner framing, assistance categories, and conditioning notes. | Use this when someone is new to 5/3/1 and wants to understand the basic structure before picking a template. |
| [T Nation: 5/3/1 - How to Build Pure Strength](https://t-nation.com/t/5-3-1-how-to-build-pure-strength/281694) | The original long-form article explaining the philosophy behind 5/3/1, its core lifts, and the slow-progress mindset. | Use this when someone wants the original rationale for the program or needs more background than the beginner summary. |
| [Jim Wendler: Boring But Big](https://www.jimwendler.com/blogs/jimwendler-com/101077382-boring-but-big) | One of the most popular 5/3/1 assistance templates, with example training weeks and notes on volume. | Use this when someone already understands basic 5/3/1 and wants a concrete hypertrophy-oriented template to run. |
| [Jim Wendler: 5/3/1 Forever](https://www.jimwendler.com/products/5-3-1-forever-book) | Official book page for the most complete programming reference in the 5/3/1 ecosystem, including main lifts, supplemental work, conditioning, mobility, and recovery. | Use this when the articles stop being specific enough and someone wants the canonical deep dive. |

## Suggested app-facing descriptions

If we need short blurbs for UI copy, these are safe defaults:

- `Workout basics`: External guides for weekly activity targets, beginner planning, and starting a routine safely.
- `Weightlifting help`: Exercise demos and conservative strength-training references for choosing and performing lifts.
- `Stretching`: Flexibility basics plus a practical library of common stretches.
- `5/3/1`: Jim Wendler resources covering the method, beginner philosophy, popular templates, and deeper programming references.
